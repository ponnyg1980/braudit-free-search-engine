2. What a Clearance Audit is

2.1 A Clearance Audit is a conflict check on a brand you intend to use or register, carried out before you apply. It helps you decide whether to go ahead, change the brand, or narrow what you apply for.

2.2 Each Clearance Audit covers one trademark: one word mark, or one image (logo) mark, for the goods and services you tell us about. A word and a logo are two audits.

2.3 What we search:

(a) UK Clearance Audit: the UK trademark register, including international registrations that have effect in the UK, together with Companies House, domain name registries and the main social media platforms for the same or similar names.

(b) Worldwide Clearance Audit: everything in (a), plus the EU trademark register, the international (WIPO Madrid) register{{COUNTRIES}}.

2.4 We compare the results with your mark for visual, phonetic and conceptual similarity and for overlap in goods and services, and give each relevant result, and the audit overall, a risk rating.

2.5 You receive a written report of our findings and recommendations. Each order includes one Clearance Consultation: a session with us to go through the results and your options.

5.1 A Clearance Audit reduces risk; it cannot remove it. Registers and public sources are never complete at every moment: recently filed applications may not yet be published, unregistered rights and use that is not online may not be visible, and a third party may object whatever the risk rating.

5.2 The risk rating is our professional opinion at the date of the report. It is not a guarantee that an application will be accepted, that no one will oppose it, or that you can use the mark without challenge. The outcome of any application depends on the Intellectual Property Office and on third parties.

5.3 The report is for you and your business, for the mark, goods and services it describes. Please do not rely on it for a different mark or different goods and services, or more than 3 months after its date without asking us to update it.

5.4 We are not responsible for conflicts arising from rights that could not reasonably be found in the sources in clause 2.3, or from information you did not give us.
