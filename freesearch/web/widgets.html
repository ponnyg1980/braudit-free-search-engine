1. Search box — Free Search variant

The compact entry point. Takes the name, mints the journey session, then hands the visitor to the full Free Search wizard on step 2 (logo & tagline).

Use it on: the home page, service pages, blog sidebars — anywhere you want the search to start without sending someone to another page first.

9. Class assistant with AI tools New

The same assistant as #7 with the AI tools grouped in their own panel and labelled as such. Reading a website pre-fills the questionnaire rather than classifying straight off the page, and the questions are written for any trade — a plumber, a builder or a charity answers them as naturally as a product brand does.

Use it on: pages where you want the smarter suggestions and are happy to ask for an email address first.

As it appears on your page
<script src="https://braudit-free-search.onrender.com/embed.js" data-widget="class-assistant-ai" data-tenant="tmh" async></script>
Fair use. Five AI calls per email address, then the tools close and the person is flagged in Zoho as AI Tools Blocked. An adviser unticking that box hands back a full five on the next call. Anyone on the Safe Email list — every @thetrademarkhelpline.com address, plus introducers and resellers ticked as AI Safe Sender — is never counted and never blocked.

What the client receives

The results page and the emailed report

The end of a search is two documents, not one. The results page is what the visitor sees the moment the search finishes — the opinion, the five closest marks, and the ask for an email address. The report is what lands in the inbox afterwards: the same opinion, every flagged mark rather than five, and each one’s owner. That withheld ownership is the whole reason to hand over an email address, so the two pages are shown here together, in the order the client meets them.

Two searches, at each end of the range. Same components in both; what changes is what the register gave back, and how bluntly the engine says it.

Crowded register — McDonald’s, classes 29, 30 & 43

24 marks flagged — 5 high risk, 13 medium — and the word mark already registered to McDonald’s International Property Company. 41%, and the headline says the quiet part out loud: that word mark is already taken — but the brand may not be. Note what it does not do: it doesn’t pretend the name is weak. Distinctiveness still scores 95. It separates the name’s own merit from whether somebody got there first, which is the conversation an adviser wants to be having.
