Advice given

Tick these only once you have actually said them.

Never say
  • Never promise registration. The IPO decides, and third parties can oppose.
  • Never promise a takedown, an outcome or a timescale you do not control.
  • Never say a search is "clear" — say nothing obvious came up on the register.
  • Never give a legal opinion on someone else's rights. That is an adviser's job.

Recommendation

What you advised, and why.

Framing the audit
  • The audit is the step that stops a wasted application. Lead with what it prevents, not what it contains.
  • It covers logos, taglines, social media, marketplaces, domains, company registers, international and prior-use risk — the free search covers none of that.
What the client hears read back, and what the paralegal delivering the audit works from — marks, classes, territories, anything specific they raised.
Your reasoning, on the record — this is the advice trail that lands on the Deal.
The delivery conversation — say it like this
  • "Our audits do use technology to run the searches, which can bring back thousands of results. We use AI technology to score the results and refine them into five ranges from high to low risk. One of our paralegals then reviews the risks and decides which results need to be in the report before presenting it back to you."
  • "We're looking at a turnaround of 3 working days at the moment, which would mean us sitting down next on … — does that work for you, or would you like me to see if I can fast track your report?"
No guard rails here — you're agreeing it live. Same day as the consultation is fine.

Consultation

The audit is delivered into a conversation, not an inbox.

Booking it properly
  • Book it on the call. This is an expected step, not an optional one — an audit with no appointment is a PDF nobody reads.
  • It is a £149 consultation they are getting free. Sell it as that, because it is that.
  • The audit must be delivered before the appointment — the date rule below enforces it, but say it out loud too.
  • "I'll get the audit to you by the {{date}}, and we'll go through it together on the {{appointment}}."
This is the exception, not a shortcut
  • Booking the appointment is the expected step in every order. Skipping it needs a very specific reason — the client's, not yours.
  • "They were busy" is not a reason. "Director is abroad until the 20th and will only book on their return" is.
  • This reason is reviewed by a manager and is recorded on the Deal.

Billing & payment

Nothing is invoiced until it is paid.

Taking payment
  • Send the payment link while they are still on the phone. A link sent after the call is a link that waits.
  • If they cannot pay today, agree an exact date — not "next week".
  • The audit does not start until payment lands, so the delivery date moves with the payment date. Say that plainly.
Billing to account
If it's a department rather than a person — accounts@, billing@ — put the department name as the first name ("Accounts", "Billing") and the last name becomes "Department".
Ask, don't assume — the address above may be a UK agent for an overseas client. VAT is charged unless the client is outside the UK.
