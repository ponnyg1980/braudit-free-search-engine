Who are we speaking to?

Take these first. Nothing else on this form opens until they are captured.

Before you go any further
  • Get the details before you give the value. If the line drops after you have talked them through their risks and you have no number, the enquiry is gone.
  • "Before we look at anything — can I take a few details so I can send this over and pick it up if we get cut off?"
  • Position matters: it tells us whether this person can actually instruct us, and it drives who signs later.
Organisation is optional — plenty of enquiries are pre-incorporation.

Discovery

Fill this in as they talk. Order does not matter.

What you are listening for
  • What do they actually sell, in their words? You will need it for the classes.
  • Are they trading yet, or pre-launch? It changes the risk conversation entirely.
  • Why now? A deadline, an investor, a letter, a rebrand — the reason drives urgency.
  • Have they had any contact from another party? If so, slow down and note it exactly.
If there is a live dispute, say you will have a specialist call them. Do not advise on it here.

What are we auditing?

Add a line for every mark. They share one set of classes and terms.

Multiple marks
  • Ask what else carries the brand — a product range, a sub-brand, a tagline they use in advertising. Each is a separate audit.
  • "Is it just the one name, or are there product names and a strapline that matter as much?"
  • Every mark is audited against the same classes and terms, so the scope is agreed once and applied to all of them.

Advice given

Tick these only once you have actually said them.

Never say
  • Never promise registration. The IPO decides, and third parties can oppose.
  • Never promise a takedown, an outcome or a timescale you do not control.
  • Never say a search is "clear" — say nothing obvious came up on the register.
  • Never give a legal opinion on someone else's rights. That is an adviser's job.

Recommendation

What you advised, and why.

Framing the audit
  • The audit is the step that stops a wasted application. Lead with what it prevents, not what it contains.
  • It covers logos, taglines, social media, marketplaces, domains, company registers, international and prior-use risk — the free search covers none of that.
  • It comes with up to an hour of consultation with a specialist.
  • "It is £99 a mark, and if you go on to apply through us we take it off your fees."

Consultation

The audit is delivered into a conversation, not an inbox.

Booking it properly
  • Book it on the call. An audit with no appointment is a PDF nobody reads.
  • The audit must be delivered before the appointment — the date rule below enforces it, but say it out loud too.
  • "I'll get the audit to you by the {{date}}, and we'll go through it together on the {{appointment}}."

Billing & payment

Nothing is invoiced until it is paid.

Taking payment
  • Send the payment link while they are still on the phone. A link sent after the call is a link that waits.
  • If they cannot pay today, agree an exact date — not "next week".
  • The audit does not start until payment lands, so the delivery date moves with the payment date. Say that plainly.

Read this back

Word for word. Then tick.

Why this exists
  • Most disputes about what was agreed come from what was assumed. Reading it back takes forty seconds and settles it.
  • If they correct something, change it here before you tick — do not tick and fix it afterwards.
