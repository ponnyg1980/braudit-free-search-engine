# tmh-scoring 2.5.0 — mark similarity reports its axes

**Released** 21 September 2026
**Band impact** ZERO DIFFERENCES on 56,690 stored records

---

## What changed

`MarkSimilarity` now carries the raw axis measurements it already computed and
threw away:

| field | is |
|---|---|
| `written` | whole-string / distinctive-core resemblance |
| `sound` | `phonetic_score` |
| `spelling` | `orthographic_score` (edit distance) |
| `token` | best distinctive-token pair |
| `axis` | which of written / sound / spelling carried the mark |

`MarkSimilarity.axes()` returns them together. `Assessment.as_row()` — what
band_diff compares and what the engine stores in `assessment` — is untouched,
which is why the diff is clean by construction as well as by measurement.

## Why

Two things needed it, and neither was possible with what was stored.

**Re-banding at a different sensitivity.** The plan for Triage sensitivity was
to re-apply bands to the stored `components` breakdown. That breakdown held the
scorer's OUTPUTS at the default settings — a mark tier, an orthographic score,
a phonetic boolean — not the raw measurements a different gate would be applied
to, and the written and token axes were not stored at all. Applying a new
threshold to an old tier is applying it to the answer rather than to the
evidence. (The audit engine now re-scores from stored inputs instead, which is
exact; these fields are what let the panel explain the result.)

**Explaining a move.** The Triage panel could say a row moved and not why.
"spelling 0.95, sound 0.86, written 0.86 — spelling carried it" is the answer
to the question staff actually ask, and it was unanswerable.

## What is deliberately NOT renamed

`MarkSimilarity.orthographic` has carried `best` — the winning score across all
axes — since before the axes were separated. Stored rows depend on it and the
existing panel reads it. `spelling` is the spelling axis proper. Both are kept;
neither is removed, and the docstring says which is which so the next reader
does not "fix" the apparent duplication.
